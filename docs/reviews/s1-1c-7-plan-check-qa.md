### What the design notes' test material must contain, per finding

- **Sub-kind:** one of K1, K2a–f, K3, K3s, K4 or K4s, as above.
- **K1:**
  - the test's name and file;
  - the assertion;
  - the revert-plant entry and the failing line expected from it;
  - for concurrency, the hold and the run under load.
- **K2:**
  - the mutate batch entry (file, exact find, replace, test, pkg, subtest);
  - the expected outcome words: old green, new red, real code green;
  - for K2b, the line that must be covered.
- **K3 and K4:**
  - the map in astequal's real key format;
  - the grep commands for each old name as a string, expected to return nothing;
  - the expected difference in the test list;
  - the line quoting the assertion that justifies each verb in a new name.
- **Deletions:** one plant per assertion, or the unchanged covered set.
- **Owner questions:** the behaviour or text the owner must decide (PQ-S12).
- **Naming check:** the golden table of names and verdicts, shared by Go and Python, and the report-only hit list compared with G3.

### Verdict

The plan is not ready for the owner. Two blockers:
- **PQ-B1:** K2 does not fit about a quarter of G2.
- **PQ-B2:** K3 and K4 do not cover the subtest, split, string-literal and Python-move shapes in G3.

With the proofs as defined, exits 2 and 3 cannot be met honestly. The should-fixes concentrate in the proof tools and plant procedure (PQ-S1, S2, S8, S13). Two contradict a recorded rule (PQ-S7, PQ-S11). The spot-check is too weak to catch drift (PQ-S9), and the ledger cannot be checked mechanically (PQ-S10). The overall structure is sound: one proof per change kind, design notes that re-check every finding, a naming check switched on red first, and a finding ledger. Once each finding has a sub-kind with a proof that can fail, the plan should be ready for a v2 review.
