## Summary

**20 findings: 2 blockers, 10 should-fix, 8 later.** Most of the note is right. These parts hold up:
- the multi-part key semantics;
- the `keyT.m` encoding (injective: each position has one type and strings carry a length prefix);
- the ordinal-keyed snapshot `Walk` (no key value leaks into it);
- the runtime-wide stamp versus each app's own `Ingest` stamp (equal by construction, since every app stamp is a router stamp);
- the map-order proof at 64 keys or more (3000 of 3000 runs printed distinct orders);
- goleak on today's tests (they pass);
- the ingest flag's zero allocations.

The weak point is the promise behind exit 11 and MUST 10: "for any live run with a recorder, replay gives the same outputs and counters". Inputs that today's `Send` and `SendPayload` accept break it:
- a typed event whose `Ts` differs from its time attribute;
- malformed UTF-8;
- object text that has whitespace or is not JSON;
- out-of-range timestamps;
- a declared `text_invisibles`;
- payloads at the decoder's nesting limit or over the reader's line limit;
- a stamp below the header's start.

Separately, the recorder runs synchronously on the router goroutine. That conflicts with ADR-0006 §5's wait discipline and with owner Q2.

## Checked and holding (no finding)

- **The multi-part map key.** `keyT{s,n,f,b}` plus `m` = the encoding of `Rest`, with kind bytes, length-prefixed strings and `-0.0 → +0.0`. It is injective per rule, because arity and per-position types are fixed. The shard hash does not need to be injective, since identity is the partition map's.
- **The snapshot `Walk`** keys instances by ordinal (`partition.go:368-381`), so a key value never becomes the walk key. `free()` recomputes `keyOf(in.Key)` (`:333`), which works if the stored `Rest` equals the lookup `Rest` (F13 keeps that true).
- **Several apps on one router.** Every app's `Ingest` stamp is fed only router stamps (`runtime.go` `ingest`/`tickApp`), so each recording's arrivals are monotone and replay's `Ingest` raises to the same values.
- **`time arrival` stamping.** Late means `ts < adm`, strictly (`clock.go:103`), so stamps that are equal or rising are never late.
- **The ingest flag and the hot path.** On the `Sync` path, a non-matching event with `IngestFlags` makes 0 allocations (`S/alloc.out`).

## Questions for the owner

A. **What should the Go entry point do with an event a recording cannot copy exactly?** Cases: its time field disagrees with its timestamp attribute, broken UTF-8 text, a JSON blob with extra spaces or invalid JSON, a date after year 9999. *Recommendation: refuse it with an error to the caller,* as malformed rows already are (S3.1-2's D2, K2). Pros: recordings always replay to the same alerts. Cons: callers that send sloppy data get errors. The alternative is to clean it quietly. Pros: nothing is refused. Cons: the event the rules see differs from what the caller sent.

B. **When the recording falls behind (a slow disk or a stalled network), should the engine wait for it, or let the recording stop?** *Recommendation: let the recording stop and report it, and never make detection wait.* This follows your Q2. The engine keeps a bounded queue in front of the recording; when the queue is full, the recording stops and says where. Pros: alerts are never late. Cons: on a slow disk a recording can end early. The alternative is to wait, as a slow alert sink does. Pros: complete recordings. Cons: every app's alerts are delayed, possibly for hours.

C. **Recordings would keep every field the sender sent, even fields no rule reads** (note open point 4). This is needed so that replay counts unknown fields and flag overrides exactly. *Recommendation: accept, and document it.* Pros: simple and exact. Cons: recordings may store data the app ignores, possibly personal data. The alternative is to record only declared fields plus explicit counts. Pros: no extra data stored. Cons: a format change of about half a point.

D. **Tripwire ceilings: one per metric (the note) or one per rule (ADR-0015 §3.11)?** *Recommendation: one per metric now, with the over-ceiling message naming the rule, and the ADR annotated.* Pros: a small file, and the zero-config rule still holds. Cons: one noisy rule cannot get its own advisory ceiling until per-rule entries are added.
