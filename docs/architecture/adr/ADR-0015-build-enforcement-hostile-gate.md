# ADR-0015: Build-time architecture enforcement — the hostile gate

## Status
Accepted (owner condition at the 2026-11-01 baseline ratification). **Revised 2026-11-01** after two adversarial reviews of this ADR itself — the gate red-team ([review/gate-redteam.md](../review/gate-redteam.md), 25 findings) and the foundation consistency review ([review/foundation-consistency.md](../review/foundation-consistency.md)) — with four owner decisions logged in [sprint-plan.md §5](../../sprints/sprint-plan.md#5-decisions): the LAW Δ law-manifest mechanism adopted; goleak + apidiff approved as dependencies (≠ recommendation); the hostility bar reworded honestly; the review-only residue (§3.6) accepted knowingly.
Mechanisms are synthesized from two of the owner's prior projects, studied 2026-11-01 and verified first-hand; they are referred to here only as **reference A** (architecture-rules-as-tests, non-vacuity doctrine) and **reference B** (registry-driven gate, planted-failure proofs, suppression accountability) and are never named in this repository.

**This ADR is the gate's single normative mechanism catalog.** DEVELOPMENT.md holds the doctrine (the four gate rules, the suppression and TODO policies) and points here; ADR-0005 §2 points here; where any other document sketches a registry schema, a tier assignment or an instrument scope that differs from this file, **this file wins** (consistency review S1/S2).

## Context
DEVELOPMENT.md pins the gate's skeleton (registry, tiers T0–T4, collect-all, zero-is-failure, watchdogs, suppression metadata, toolchain parity). ARCHITECTURE.md §2b names eight review-blocking quality rules, the first four mechanically gated. This ADR is the missing half: **which mechanisms make those words executable**, such that (a) every architectural constraint has an instrument or is named in the review-only residue (§3.6), (b) every instrument is proven able to fail, and (c) the sum meets the owner's bar, honestly stated (§3.7): *every freestyle shortcut is either mechanically red or a named loud row at merge.* Reference A's doctrine governs: *"an invariant without an instrument is a claim, not a property"*; reference B's governs the gate: *"a check that can't fail isn't a check."*

The red-team's root finding (GR-01) shapes this revision: **every rule the gate enforces lives in repo files the policed code's authors can edit**, and the merge review is the only remaining net. The gate therefore cannot be self-protecting; its design goal is that no law change is ever *silent* (§1.3), and the floor of the whole design is stated plainly: **the owner reads the LAW Δ block at every merge** (GR-12 accepted honestly).

## Decision

### 1. One registry, one runner, one law manifest

**1.1 Registry and runner (reference B).** `gate/registry.json` rows are executed by `gate/run.sh` identically from hooks, `make check|combine-check|strict-gate`, and CI. The row schema — **the single normative statement; DEVELOPMENT.md and ADR-0005 cite it**:

```
id            unique instrument/step id (string)
job           the CI job that runs it
tiers         subset of {T0,T1,T2,T3,T4}
blocking      true | false (false = advisory; advisory rows age, §3.9)
timeout       per-step watchdog (TERM then KILL, process group)
needs         ids that must complete first (DAG, validated by registry-lint)
scope         file classes the step sees: subset of {prod, test, bench, tools, proofs, generated} (§2.1)
expected_scope a `git ls-files` glob or `go list` pattern; the runner expands it LIVE and
              fails when the step's covered-count ≠ the expansion (GR-13 — "n ≥ 1" is not a floor)
min_wants     analyzers only: the minimum number of `// want` expectations in the
              analyzer's fixture; the teeth meta-test asserts it (§3.8, GR-04)
lands_in      the sprint that builds this row (§2.2, GR-20)
command       what runs
```

*Terminology (consistency review S1):* the anti-vacuity check above is the row's **expected scope**; the word "coverage" is reserved for the statement-coverage instrument (`coverage-inclusion`). The two are different mechanisms and are never conflated again.

**1.2 Registry-lint** (itself a row): every row's CI job exists in the workflow YAML; every `needs` names a real row and the graph is acyclic; timeouts are well-formed; each CI job's timeout ≥ the sum of its rows' caps **+ 20%, minimum 60 s margin** (pinned — GR-23); every id in this ADR's §2 table exists in the registry, `blocking: true`, at its stated tiers, once its `lands_in` sprint has merged (GR-11 — deleting or demoting a row trips registry-lint, not just review). The CI summary job (`needs: all`, `if: always()`, the ONLY required check) turns a missing/stale/duplicate/malformed per-step record, or a skipped job, into a **named RED row** — a skipped gate is never a passed gate. The summary logic ships with fixture trees (planted problems) it must classify correctly. **Branch protection (the required-check name, no force-push to `main`) is owner-side GitHub state the repository cannot grant itself; it is recorded in the sprint record, and its absence is a standing known-issue, never assumed** (GR-12).

**1.3 The law manifest and the LAW Δ block (GR-01 — the systemic countermeasure; owner-adopted 2026-11-01).**
`gate/law.json` enumerates every **law path** — the files whose content *is* the rules: `gate/` (registry.json, run.sh, the summary logic, regen-baseline, law.json itself), `.github/workflows/`, `tools.json`, `tools/deps-allowlist.json`, `tools/allowlists/*.json` (§3.10), `tools/importcheck/rules.json`, `tools/analyzers/**` (sources **and** `testdata/` fixtures), `api/exports.golden`, `docs/language/grammar.lark`, `docs/language/grammar.ebnf`, `docs/language/SPEC.md`, `docs/language/SEMANTICS.md`, `docs/language/errors.md`, `docs/architecture/ARCHITECTURE.md`, `docs/architecture/adr/`.
An always-on row (`lawdelta`, every tier, never skipped) diffs the law paths against the **merge base with `main`** and prints a **`LAW Δ` block at the top of the summary table**: per changed file — path, +/− line counts, and for structured files a one-line semantic delta ("rules.json: +1 edge `expr → runtime`"; "clockcheck allowlist: +`internal/pattern`"; "exports.golden: +3 exports"; "SPEC.md touched — **the spec is frozen; a change requires an owner decision**", GR-14). The row is **YELLOW, never blocking** (law must remain changeable) and **never absent** — an empty delta prints `LAW Δ: none`. The owner's check (sprint model §8 step 10) carries the standing line: **"LAW Δ block read and intended."** This converts the gate's worst defeat class — a self-consistent edit of code + rule in one commit, green everywhere — into a handful of loud lines in the one place the owner already reads (16 of the red-team's 25 findings reduce to this hole; GR-02/03/04/05/11/12/14 are closed or contained by it).

**1.4 Law-file ownership (GR-18).** The coordinator owns `gate/`, `tools/*.json`, `tools/allowlists/` and `tools/importcheck/rules.json` in every sprint's lane roster. Agents never edit law files in their lanes; a lane that needs a law delta (a new allowlist entry, a registry change) states it in its lane report and the coordinator applies it on the integration branch, where the `LAW Δ` block then shows the sprint's law changes as one reviewed set.

### 2. Architecture constraints as analyzers with teeth (reference A, in Go)

**2.1 Scope classes and the generated-code policy (GR-07/GR-09/GR-19, consistency B6/S9 — decided now, not discovered as friction).**
Every first-party file belongs to exactly one class; each registry row's `scope` says which classes the step sees, and registry-lint asserts the row's command matches:

- **prod** — library and binary code.
- **test** — `*_test.go` and `testdata/`. `funlen`, `gocyclo`, the file ≤ 500 and params limits **do not apply** here (table-driven tests are the project's idiom; GR-09); `errcheck`, `gocritic`, `forbidigo`, import and clock rules still do.
- **bench** — `bench/`. Out of scope for `clockcheck` and `testhygiene` (ADR-0010 §8's open-loop generator MUST pace on the wall clock); in scope for `importcheck`.
- **tools** — `tools/`, `gate/` scripts. Gated by `smells` and `testhygiene` only.
- **proofs** — `gate/proofs/` (§3.4). Deliberately defective code; out of scope for every analyzer; runs only under the T4 proof rows.
- **generated** — any file whose first line matches the canonical header `^// Code generated .* DO NOT EDIT\.$`. **Exempt** from `smells`, `globalscheck`, and excluded from the coverage numerator **and** denominator; **in scope** for `importcheck`, `clockcheck`, `puritycheck` (a generated file importing `os` is still a bug). Anti-forgery (GR-07): `freshness` fails if any file bearing the header is not in its regeneration manifest — a forged header on hand-written code is caught because nothing regenerates it. Generators (`goyacc` wrapper, `kwgen`) MUST emit the header.

**2.2 The instrument table.** Every instrument is a `go/analysis` analyzer (or a `go test` scanning via `go/packages`), each shipping an `analysistest` fixture seeding the exact defect it exists to catch, with its `min_wants` floor pinned in its registry row (§3.8). **`lands_in` is law (GR-20):** an instrument lands in the sprint that creates its first subject, *with* its fixture, its registry row, and its id added to the teeth pin-list in the same commit — the gate's own zero-is-failure rule forbids landing subject-less instruments. When an instrument lands after code it governs already exists (e.g. `globalscheck` in S2 over S1's kernels), its first run scans the whole module retroactively — nothing is grandfathered.

### 3. Hostility (what makes freestyle coding loud or red)

1. **T0 pre-commit** (installed once via `core.hooksPath .githooks`; a convenience, not the net — CI + the human merge are the net): parity check on analyzer major versions (hard fail naming the fix), auto-format then **re-stage only the exact paths it formatted, via `git add --` on that list** (GR-17a — never a blanket re-stage), then the **six** staged-file T0 instruments: `importcheck`, `clockcheck`, `puritycheck`*, `globalscheck`*, `todocheck`, `smells` (*once landed per their `lands_in`; the count "four" in the previous revision was wrong — GR-23). A missing tool is a **hard fail, not a skip**. Hooks are technically bypassable (`--no-verify` exists); **policy forbids using it** (DEVELOPMENT.md, sprint model §10 — one statement, no contradiction: the hook is convenience, CI is the net, so a bypassed hook changes nothing downstream).
2. **Toolchain exactness**: `GOTOOLCHAIN=local`, `go.mod` `toolchain` line, Go 1.24 `tool` directives hash-pinned in `go.sum`, `tools.json` for non-Go tools (incl. Python + `lark==1.3.1` for the grammar-differential) with sha256 verified **every run**; "never loosen a pin to pass"; a bump is one commit that also fixes what the new version finds. The installer writes into `cache/tmp.$$` and **atomically `mv`s** into the hash-keyed path — `mv` on one filesystem is the lock; no flock dependency (macOS has none; GR-17b). A weekly advisory canary runs latest tools and opens/updates ONE issue (never blocks; ages per §3.9).
3. **Expected scope everywhere** (GR-13): formatter/linter file counts must equal the `git ls-files` expansion of the row's `expected_scope`; every scanning step prints `GATE_COVERED n` and the runner compares n against the **live expansion**, failing with both numbers named. A scope-narrowed command no longer passes by printing 1.
4. **Planted-failure proofs for the gate itself** (GR-25 — mechanism now stated): `gate/proofs/` is a **separate Go module** (own `go.mod`), never imported by the engine, compiled and run **only** by the T4 proof rows, each of which asserts two things: the instrument fired on its planted defect, AND `go test -json` ran ≥ 1 test (a typo'd exclusion that silences a proof is itself red). Proofs: the race step catches a planted race; the leak step (goleak, owner-approved) catches a planted leak; registry-lint reports its three planted problems with exact messages; freshness catches a planted stale file; the two-OS byte-diff catches a planted map-iteration nondeterminism. **Build-tag law**: no first-party file outside `gate/proofs/` may carry a `//go:build` constraint not on the pinned list in `gate/law.json` (OS tags where justified; never `!race`) — a tagged-out file is invisible to `go/packages` analyzers, so tags are law.
5. **Failure UX is a contract**: every blocking step's failure output names the fix command or the exact doc section (reference B's style); the gate's summary table — LAW Δ block first — is the single thing a human or agent reads.
6. **The §2b mapping and the review-only residue (owner accepted 2026-11-01 — consistency S11).** ARCHITECTURE §2b rules 1–4 map to `importcheck`/`clockcheck`/`puritycheck`+`globalscheck`. The following are **review-only** — no analyzer can see them; the architect review of every combined diff carries them, with `apipartition` and `smells` as mechanical outriders. Recorded verbatim so the gap is accepted, not discovered:
   1. §2b rule 5 — an operator hand-computing watermarks/deadlines/ordering from event timestamps (no banned API is involved; the clock conformance ports are a partial net);
   2. §2b rule 6 — gratuitous exports from `internal/*` (`apipartition` guards only the public surface);
   3. §2b rule 7 — rule- or example-keyed special-case branches (no analyzer knows intent);
   4. §2b rule 8 — copy-paste between operators vs premature framework;
   5. ADR-0003 §1 — "a model field with no SPEC clause is forbidden" (docsync checks packages/errors/functions/keywords, not field↔clause);
   6. DEVELOPMENT.md — "the hot path never blocks" and "adapters stay thin" (puritycheck's import bans and the saturation test are partial);
   7. the layer charter's "MUST NOT know about" beyond what imports reveal.
7. **The bar, honestly stated (owner decision 2026-11-01, replacing "effectively impossible to code against by hand"):** *every freestyle shortcut is either mechanically red, or a named loud row (LAW Δ, API Δ, suppression/allowlist delta) in the summary the owner reads at merge.* The previous overclaim was itself a risk: the human checkpoint relaxes in proportion to the gate's advertised strength (GR-24).
8. **Teeth, strengthened (GR-04/GR-11):** the meta-test (`tools/analyzers/teeth_test.go`) enumerates analyzers and fails on any missing or orphaned fixture, **asserts each analyzer's fixture contains ≥ its registry row's `min_wants` expectations** (weakening an analyzer now requires lowering a pinned number in a law file → LAW Δ), and **pins the §2 instrument id list** (each id present in the registry, blocking, at its stated tiers, per its `lands_in`).
9. **Advisory aging (GR-22):** every advisory finding carries its first-seen date in the run record; an advisory row older than **2 sprints** turns blocking (or is dispositioned by an owner decision first). The weekly canary issue and perf-band notes are advisory rows, so they age too — nothing yellow becomes wallpaper.
10. **One allowlist grammar (GR-05):** every allowlist (`clockcheck`, `puritycheck` named exceptions, `globalscheck`, forbidigo-`panic`, `coverage-inclusion`, `tools/deps-allowlist.json`) lives under `tools/allowlists/*.json` in one format — `{target, kind: PERMANENT|TEMP, reason (≥ 12 chars), ref (required for PERMANENT, resolved), expiry|issue (required for TEMP), sprint}` — checked by the suppressions checker with the same liveness rule (an entry naming a nonexistent package/rule fails). All allowlists are law-manifest files; the summary prints each list's count and delta. **The allowlist waives a package's inclusion, never an included package's floor.**
11. **Metric tripwires are zero-config-is-failure (GR-15, consistency S7):** the T4 tripwire step fails with "no ceiling configured for <metric>/<rule>" when a tracked metric lacks a ceiling; a deliberate opt-out is an explicit `ceiling: none # reason` entry in the law-manifested config. The **outbox ceiling is blocking** with default 65,536 per ADR-0006 §5 (qa-strategy §4 corrected); `partial-count`, `sink-depth` and `drained_pending` are advisory per-rule ceilings set at the plan check, aging per §3.9.

### 4. What we deliberately do NOT adopt (differences argued, not drifted into)

- **No mutation-testing tool** (neither reference uses one). Stated honestly (GR-06 — the previous "same assurance" claim was false): the planted proofs prove **the gate's instruments** can fail, not that **product tests** can fail. Product-test assertion strength is guaranteed by a different mechanism: testing.md's first-seen-failing rule, the QA agent's independently written failing test at each plan check (sprint model §6/§8), and red-run evidence quoted in the PR. The whole-engine break-it before each release MAY add a sampled spot-check (flip one constant per package; the suite must go red) — mutation testing scoped to where it matters, by hand.
- **No commit-message linting** (neither reference): the sprint model's one-PR-per-sprint and human merge govern history shape.
- **No coverage theater**: the 85% floor is a tripwire under the binary inclusion rule, not the goal; inclusion is binary (allowlist-waivable with a recorded reason), the floor is modest and unwaivable within its stated scope (§2.2), and assertion-free tests are called out as the residue they are (§4 first bullet — coverage proves execution, never correctness).
- **Dependencies (owner-approved 2026-11-01, ≠ recommendation; ADR-0009 amended):** `go.uber.org/goleak` (test-only, the leak proofs) and `golang.org/x/exp/apidiff` (build-time only, T4 API diff); `golang.org/x/tools` scope widened to include `go/analysis` + `analysistest` for the gate's analyzers. All pinned in `tools/deps-allowlist.json`.

## Consequences
- Positive: every architectural sentence either names its instrument or stands in the §3.6 residue the owner accepted — no third state; law changes are structurally loud (LAW Δ); drift between docs, registry, CI and code is itself a red build; agents receive failures that teach the fix.
- Negative: the gate is a real subsystem with its own proofs, **spread over S1–S3 by the `lands_in` column** (GR-20 — the previous "most of sprint S1" budget was impossible against ADR-0005's Stage 1; every instrument now has a named sprint and lands with its first subject); analyzer false positives cost accountable suppressions or allowlist entries (both loud), never rule deletions.
- Accepted residual: with one human merger, the floor of the design is the owner reading the LAW Δ block (§1.3). The gate is hostile, not self-protecting — stated plainly by decision.

## Implementation notes for coding agents
- Analyzers live in `tools/analyzers/<name>/` with `testdata/` fixtures (`// want` comments); the meta-test is `tools/analyzers/teeth_test.go`; each analyzer's `min_wants` is in its registry row.
- `gate/run.sh` is POSIX shell, per-step process groups (`set -m`), TERM-then-KILL watchdogs, JSON record per step, `summary.md` (LAW Δ block first) + `run.json`, `::error` annotations in CI.
- `api/exports.golden` format: one line per export — `package ident kind signature` — sorted; regenerating it is editing law (LAW Δ + review).
- S1 build order: registry + runner + summary (collect-all, zero-is-failure, LAW Δ row) → tools.json/parity/atomic installer → registry-lint → `importcheck` + `clockcheck` with fixtures and the teeth meta-test → pinned golangci `smells` with the §2.1 scopes → suppression grammar + `nolintlint` → `todocheck` → hooks → CI wiring + summary fixtures. Everything else lands per its `lands_in` with its subject.
- The scratch directory for `freshness` is the runner's per-step temp dir, never the worktree.
